⚔ The Dungeon

A vault lies under the campus. Four floors stand between you and it.

Tip

How to play. Every door below is a collapsible section. Click one to open it. There is exactly one way down from each floor, and every floor can be solved by reading carefully rather than guessing. Each correct door yields one digit of the vault code. Collect four, open the vault, take what is inside.

Choosing wrong kills you, but death is cheap here. Every ending has a way back.


Floor I · The Gate of Three

Important

Three gates stand in the rock. Carved above them, in a hand long dead: "Only the gate that never sleeps will admit the living."

🚪  The Gate of Iron  ·  "I rest when the sun sets."


Caution

The iron gate opens onto nothing at all, and you fall for what feels like a sprint review. It rested, exactly as promised. So, now, do you.

↺ back to the gate

🚪  The Gate of Bone  ·  "I rest when the moon rises."


No spikes. No pit. A corridor, sloping gently downward. This feels, briefly, like progress.

It is not progress. But you were never going to learn that standing still.

The corridor ends at two unmarked doors.

🚪  The left door


Caution

The moon rose an hour ago. The gate behind you fell asleep, as promised, and sealed the corridor. The left door opens onto solid mountain.

A gate that rests is a gate that traps. The inscription was not decoration.

↺ back to the gate

🚪  The right door


Caution

The right door opens onto the left door. The left door opens onto the right door. You spend some time on this. The moon sets. The moon rises.

Not every branch that goes deeper is the branch that goes forward.

↺ back to the gate

🚪  The Gate of Flame  ·  "I burn at dawn and I burn at dusk."


Tip

The gate opens. Something that burns at dawn and at dusk never rests at all. Heat rolls over you without burning. Beyond it, stairs descend.

Floor II · The Hall of Three Chests

Important

Three chests. One holds the descending key, the others hold something with teeth. Exactly one of these engravings tells the truth.

  • Chest A — "The key is NOT in chest C."
  • Chest B — "The key is in THIS chest."
  • Chest C — "The key is NOT in chest B."

📦  Open chest A


Caution

If the key were in A, then A's engraving would be true, and so would C's. That is two truths, and you were promised exactly one. A was never possible.

↺ back to the gate

📦  Open chest B


Caution

If the key were in B, then B's engraving would be true, and so would A's. Two truths again. The chest, which was lying, resents being doubted.

↺ back to the gate

📦  Open chest C


Tip

The key was in chest C, the only arrangement leaving exactly one honest engraving.

Floor III · The Bridge and the Keeper

Important

A chasm, a rope bridge, and something enormous sitting cross-legged in the middle of it.

"I have keys but open no locks. I have space but no room. You may enter, but you may not go in. Name me and pass."

🗝  "A map."


Caution

"A map has no keys," rumbles the Keeper. "Only a legend. As do I. Fall well."

↺ back to the gate

🗝  "A coffin."


Caution

"Morbid," says the Keeper, mildly impressed. "And wrong. You may enter a coffin, and you very much do go in." It demonstrates.

↺ back to the gate

🗝  "A keyboard."


Tip

The Keeper steps aside. Keys, a space bar, and an enter key.

"Most adventurers say a piano," it admits. "You are the first to notice the ENTER."

Floor IV · The Lich of Infinite Loops

Important

A figure of dust and stack traces rises from a throne of deprecated dependencies. It has been here since the first while(true).

"A thousand engineers have shouted at me and I have never once relented. Only a SILENT LISTENER has ever moved me. Choose your weapon."

⚔  The mechanical keyboard +2


Caution

You type furiously at the Lich. It reads your code, laughs a dry O(n²) laugh, and hands back a stack trace forty frames deep. This was never a silent weapon.

↺ back to the gate

⚔  The Ctrl+Z amulet


Caution

The amulet flares, and fails. Single use. Already used. The Lich does not even move.

↺ back to the gate

⚔  The rubber duck


Tip

Critical hit.

You set the duck down and explain the problem out loud, from the beginning, slowly. Halfway through your own sentence you stop. You see it. It was the loop condition. It was always the loop condition.

The Lich crumbles. It had been waiting a very long time for someone to simply listen.
